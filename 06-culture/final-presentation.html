The decision

Should Ascend IQ ship?

Decision: HOLD. I recommend holding Ascend IQ’s enterprise launch because our evals identified critical integration misrepresentations, and we have not yet demonstrated that the release candidate fixes them and passes the required Hard gate.

Module 6
Ship-or-hold memo
The arguments

1. Critical misinformation puts enterprise purchasing decisions and client trust at risk.. Evidence: The taxonomy identifies 3 P0 failure modes. Their shared #HALLUCINATION tag appears in 10/20 audited answers (50%); this is the tag’s frequency, not each individual failure’s frequency.
2. Detecting failures does not demonstrate that the product has fixed them.. Evidence: The judge caught 2/2 integration-misrepresentation cases, while each rule caught 1/2. The release candidate’s post-fix failure rate is not measured yet.

Next step

Please approve holding the enterprise launch and dedicating the next sprint to fixing the P0 failures and validating the release candidate against our Hard gates. Approve this before next sprint planning; reconsider launch once the fixes and passing gate results are documented.

Module 1
Evaluation strategy
User promise

For VP-level strategists and product leaders, Ascend IQ promises to deliver accurate market insights in seconds, backed by verified sources, so that they can make informed decisions faster and confidently rely on Ascend Analytics.

Three trust metrics

Latency
Hallucination Rate
Robustness
Why: Hallucination Rate: Supports “accurate insights backed by verified sources” by measuring false or fabricated claims that could mislead decisions.

Trade-offs

Hallucination Rate ↔ Latency: We prioritize reducing hallucination rate over minimizing latency because clients rely on Ascend IQ for important business decisions, and a slower, verified answer is preferable to a fast, misleading one.
Latency ↔ Robustness: We prioritize robustness over minimizing latency because clients need reliable handling of ambiguous or challenging questions, even when validation, retries, or clarification take additional time.

Insights

What I learned

Where the course was hardest, what you’ll keep doing, and the moment your thinking changed.

Friction

The hardest part was separating product failures from evaluator mistakes and deciding which risks should block launch. I worked through it by comparing answers with verified sources, challenging the judge’s calls, and tying each gate to a specific business impact.

Key learnings

I’ll keep defining quality before building AI features, turning failures into business risks, and setting release gates with explicit owners and thresholds. After launch, I’ll use live-traffic evaluations to discover new failures and add them to the golden set.
